# 3D and film

Two kinds of depth, both exported from `src/kit`, both steered by one camera helper. Reach for them
when the idea needs space: a journey through the product, a logo that should feel like an object,
the product's screens as places. Not to decorate a scene that works flat.

## When 3D earns its place

- **Film form** (style.md, "Two forms"): one continuous shot through the product's world.
  `Flythrough` is its core scene.
- **The logo as an object** at the big moment of a launch: `LogoReveal`, or `Logo3D` in your own
  scene.
- **The product's own shapes in depth**, as your signature moment: its cards stacked in space, its
  board seen from a low angle, its screens arranged as a room, its data as solid bars.
- **The person asks** for 3D, "cinematic", "like a movie", "like an Apple ad".

One camera move at a time still holds: the camera glides, lands and rests while words are read.
Nothing spins, wobbles or floats for its own sake.

## The camera

```tsx
const camera = cameraAt(frame, [
  { at: 0, position: [-4.8, -2.2, 3.4], target: [0, 0.9, 0] },   // low, from the side
  { at: 110, position: [0, 0.35, 7.4], target: [0, 0.35, 0] }    // lands facing it
])
```

`position` is where the camera is, `target` what it looks at: x to the right, y up, z toward the
viewer. `fov` (default 35°) is how wide it sees. Between keys it eases in and out (pass an easing
as the third argument to change that); after the last key it holds. Frames are the scene's own.

## Space: the page in depth

`Space` places ordinary scene elements (screenshots, the rebuilt product UI, cards, words) in 3D
and flies the camera through them. They stay sharp and in the brand's fonts, and everything that
works in a flat scene works inside a `Place`. Use it for anything that is the product's UI.

```tsx
<Space camera={camera}>
  <Place at={[-900, 0, 0]} turn={14} width={1440} height={900}>
    <Board />                      {/* any element, drawn at 1440×900 */}
  </Place>
  <Place at={[900, 0, -2200]} turn={-14} width={1440} height={900}>…</Place>
</Space>
```

Units are pixels: at `z = 0`, seen from `[0, 0, focalLength(height)]`, a 1440-wide element is
drawn 1440 px wide. `turn` turns an element's face to the right (degrees), `tilt` tips its top
away. Things fade into the canvas with distance (`fog: [start, gone]`, default 2.4 and 6 focal
lengths) and vanish before the camera would pass through them. Put words that must be read in a
`Caption` over the `Space`, not inside it.

## Stage3D: solid objects

`Stage3D` is a three.js scene (React Three Fiber) over the video's canvas, lit like a quiet studio
(darker and harder in the technical look). Use it for things that need light and thickness.

```tsx
<Stage3D camera={camera}>
  <group position={[0, 1, 0]}>
    <Logo3D size={1.7} />
  </group>
  <mesh position={[0, -1, 0]}>
    <boxGeometry args={[3, 0.2, 2]} />
    <meshStandardMaterial color={colors.accent} roughness={0.5} />
  </mesh>
</Stage3D>
```

- `Logo3D` is the brand's SVG logo extruded into a solid, in its own colours; on the bold canvas
  (or dark, without `LogoOnDark`) it stands on a white tile, like `BrandMark`. A logo that isn't
  an SVG shows flat.
- `useImageTexture(src)` loads a screenshot under public/ as a texture and holds the render until
  it has.
- Colours come from `useBrand()`, as everywhere. Brand colours render as given (no tone curve), so
  keep lights moderate: strong reflections wash the brand colour out.
- Units are free: `Logo3D` is 2 across by default, and a camera 6–8 away frames it.

## Rules that keep 3D rendering right

- **Everything moves from `useCurrentFrame()`**: positions, rotations and the camera are functions
  of the frame. Never `useFrame`, a clock, `Math.random()` without a seed, or physics that step
  over time: each frame is rendered on its own, possibly in another order, and must come out the
  same every time.
- **No animation libraries with their own clock** (GSAP, anime.js, framer-motion's timers): they
  run in real time, not in frames. Their curves are easy to rebuild with `progress`, `spring` and
  `cameraAt`. If a developer asks for GSAP, a paused timeline seeked to `frame / fps` works, but
  the kit's helpers are simpler.
- **3D renders in software** inside the video app, so a heavy scene slows the whole render: keep
  meshes modest (a few thousand triangles, no huge textures), and check timing on a full render.
- **Look at stills** of every 3D scene at its start, mid-move and resting frame, in every shape;
  a camera that clips an object or leaves the frame empty only shows there.

## Kit scenes

| Scene | Use it for | Props |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Flythrough` | The film form's shot: screens hang in space receding into the distance, the camera opens wide on all of them, then flies to each in turn and settles while its line appears below | `stops: { image? \| visual?, caption, detail?, accent? }[]` (2–5), `viewport?` |
| `LogoReveal` | The logo as a solid, the camera gliding round from a low angle to face it, name and tagline beneath | `tagline?`, `taglineAccent?` |

A `Flythrough` stop's `visual` is any element drawn at `viewport` size (default 1440×900), such as
the product's rebuilt screen; its `useCurrentFrame()` counts from the moment the camera sets off
toward it, so its rows and cards have landed by the time the camera settles.
